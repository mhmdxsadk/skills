# Optional discovery and validation

Purpose: improve the evidence for a consequential decision when useful, without making validation a prerequisite for planning.

## Choose whether more evidence is useful

Reuse supplied research, first-hand experience, analytics, prior experiments, and existing product material. Identify what each source actually supports, its context and limitations, and whether it still applies. Do not repeat research merely to fill a stage.

Separate uncertainty about the problem, broader demand, usability, technical feasibility, and commercial viability. Evidence for one does not establish the others. A builder's own recurring problem can justify a personal tool while leaving demand for a SaaS version unproven.

Recommend additional validation only when the result could change the next investment or when the user wants it. Offer a focused proposal, reuse sufficient evidence, or continue without new validation at the user's choice. Record skipped or deferred checks and their implications without repeatedly asking the user to reconsider.

## Define the smallest useful check

Prioritize assumptions by the consequence of being wrong and the uncertainty in the available evidence. Avoid arbitrary confidence scores. For a selected assumption, propose:

- The decision it affects and what would change the recommendation.
- A suitable method, such as observing the existing workflow, reviewing supplied usage data, comparing alternatives, interviewing intended users, or planning a focused prototype.
- The observation to collect, whose situation it represents, and material sampling limitations.
- A stopping point and an interpretation of supportive, contradictory, or inconclusive results. Agree meaningful thresholds where possible; do not invent demand targets.

Keep the method proportional. An interview may reveal workflow problems but does not establish willingness to pay; a working integration sample does not establish production reliability. A prototype can test a narrow question without constituting an MVP.

Research public material when authorized and available, citing sources and distinguishing observations from inference. Do not fabricate interviews, analytics, market estimates, or test results. Planning interviews, outreach, purchases, experiments, or prototypes is distinct from executing them; stay within the task's authorization. When evidence cannot be collected, describe the limitation and continue independent planning.

## Interpret and return to the decision

Look for contradictory evidence and plausible alternative explanations, not only support for the initial idea. Explain how well the evidence matches the intended users and context. Treat inconclusive results as uncertainty, not automatic rejection or validation.

Adapt the [evidence and validation template](../templates/evidence-and-validation.md), or add a short evidence section to the existing brief. Record what was planned versus actually performed, what was learned, and which assumptions remain open.

Recommend PROCEED, VALIDATE, REVISE, PARK, or KILL with reasons and the next action. Recommendations to stop need more than missing information. The user owns the decision; a choice to proceed without more evidence is compatible with further planning. Update affected problem or scope decisions, then return to the workflow rather than allowing research to expand indefinitely.
